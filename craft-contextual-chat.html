02 · Users and insight3 roles18 interviews

Three roles. Same broken loop.

Everyone started broad. Then the product made them translate a hunch into exact filter vocabulary before they'd seen anything. That's where they got stuck.

Investor

VC or angel investor

Reviews 30 to 50 companies a week. Thesis, search, shortlist, partner meeting.

  • Can't express a thesis as a search query
  • 45 minutes per company across 5 to 7 tabs
  • Misses deals because research takes longer than the round
Analyst

Investment analyst

Owns a sector and turns findings into a partner-ready brief, often overnight.

  • Copies data from profiles into spreadsheets by hand
  • Profiles have no shared structure, so comparing is slow
  • Every brief starts from scratch
VC or PE team

Researcher on a fund team

Screens hundreds of companies and watches lists for new signals.

  • AI answers had no sources, so they couldn't be trusted
  • No watchlist or portfolio-level view
  • Filters for EBITDA and margins were imprecise or missing

The insight: the interface asked for precision before investors had it.

03 · Process and decisions2-week sprint3 concepts

The team had two obvious answers: better filters, or a chatbot. Filters without intelligence stay rigid. Chat without context turns generic. So I didn't jump to chat. We explored three directions fully first.

We didn't build a chatbot. We built a system that understands context.

Explored, rejected

Smarter filters

Filter groups, saved templates and tooltips. Familiar and fast to ship.

Filters demand precision. Our users didn't have it yet.

Parked for phase 2

AI dashboard

Home-screen picks from portfolio and behaviour signals.

Good for coming back. Bad for first discovery: new investors have no signals and "magic" picks aren't trusted.

Chosen

Conversational AI

Plain language mapped to company data in real time.

Investors explore before they can specify. Chat mirrors that.

Usability testing2 rounds6 then 8 people

I tested to break it, not to confirm it.

Round 1 was an unmoderated Figma test with 6 internal users in week 3. Round 2 was 45-minute think-aloud sessions with 8 investors on staging in week 6.

  1. Critical

    The empty input froze people

    4 of 6 users stalled for 15+ seconds at "Ask anything".

    Changed: 3 prompt starters by role, and an empty state that reads as an invitation.

  2. High

    Prose answers got skipped

    Investors wanted to scan in under 10 seconds. They jumped straight to the company list.

    Changed: a bold summary line, then bullets. Tables for comparisons.

  3. High

    "New Chat" felt like losing work

    3 users hesitated. One said: "If I start over, I lose everything I've done."

    Changed: renamed to "Start new thread", with a note that history is saved.

  4. Medium

    Insights felt cut off from the data

    People liked the summary but couldn't fact-check it.

    Changed: every claim links to the tab where its data lives.

06 · What I learnedLooking backAnd next

Wall-of-text answers were correct and useless. Early chat repeated the obvious. Too much AI freedom made results feel random. Each of those pushed me toward more structure.

The big call wasn't the chat UI. It was reframing "better search" as "lower cost of forming a view."

What worked

  • Mapping the funnel before any UI. The real jam was evaluation, not discovery.
  • Keeping keyword search as a fallback. Nobody was forced into chat on day one.
  • Sources were never optional. Professionals need the reasoning.

What I'd change

  • Test the first chat earlier. It was hard even for experts, and it cost a full iteration.
  • Ideate with 2 or 3 investors, not just research with them.
  • Define "AI confidence" as a metric from day one. It only became one midway.

Next

  • Proactive alerts on followed companies
  • Prompts tuned by role
  • Portfolio-level analysis
  • Shared chats and notes
